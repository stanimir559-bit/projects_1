Progress & Overload Guide

How to track, when to increase, and what to expect

Progressive Overload Rules
✓
When to add weight
Complete all prescribed sets at the TOP of the rep range with clean form, with at least 1 rep left in the tank. Then add 2.5kg next session.
✓
When NOT to add weight
You struggled on the last set, or you had to compromise form. Repeat the same weight next session. Progress is measured in weeks, not days.
✓
Deload every 6–8 weeks
Reduce all weights by ~40% but keep same number of sets. This is active recovery — not a week off. It prevents accumulated fatigue from stalling progress.
✓
Track every session
Write down every exercise, weight, sets, and reps. Without this, you're guessing. Use any app or a notebook. Date every entry.

Key Rules for Your Situation
①Don't chase the scale. At your stats with your training volume, muscle gain and fat loss will happen simultaneously. The scale might not move for 3–4 weeks while your body is recomping. This is success, not failure.
②Sleep is mandatory. With fasted training, high activity, and a deficit, you absolutely need 7.5–8h. Your Magnesium Citrate + Ashwagandha stack should help significantly. Poor sleep tanks testosterone and cortisol spikes undo recomp.
③Don't add more cardio. Your current activity level (4× gym + daily cycling + 8-10k steps) is already substantial. Adding more cardio to speed up fat loss will increase muscle catabolism in a deficit. The cycling you do is sufficient and optimal.
④Protein is the priority. If you're going to be imprecise on one macro, let it be carbs or fat. Never let protein drop below 170g on any day. Your 519-kcal breakfast already gives you 46g — you're starting every day from a strong base.
⑤Weekend flexibility. You don't need to track perfectly 7 days a week. Hit your protein and calorie targets Mon–Fri strictly. On weekends, eat intuitively but keep protein high. This is sustainable and won't derail your progress.
⑥Re-evaluate after 8 weeks. If the scale has dropped more than 3kg and strength is falling, eat more. If scale hasn't moved and progress photos show no change, reduce by another 100–150 kcal. Stay flexible.
